# Feeding-guide source review

Reviewed October 13, 2026. This is a small, source-backed educational sample, not a Cavite species inventory.

| Taxon | Identity source | Diet source and scope | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Channa striata* (snakehead; locally called dalag) | WoRMS AphiaID 280130. | Paglinawan et al. (2022), wild specimens from three municipalities around Lake Mainit, Caraga. Abstract, results, Table 1 and discussion describe shrimp, fish, crab, plant debris, detritus, aquatic insects and snails; shrimp had the highest IRI in the two sampled seasons. | Publish a qualified summary with study location and seasonal/sample caveat. The source does not establish a Cavite diet. |
| *Oreochromis niloticus* (Nile tilapia) | WoRMS AphiaID 293639. | Richter et al. (2001), repository abstract only, caged Nile tilapia at Laguna de Bay sampled in 1995. The abstract reports mostly *Anabaena spiroides* in May and mainly detritus as natural food in August. | Publish only those qualified study findings; no universal tilapia diet claim. |

[Article](https://journal.usep.edu.ph/index.php/Southeastern_Philippines_Journal/article/download/135/76), DOI: 10.53899/spjrd.v27i2.1352.
- Richter H, Focken U, Becker K, Santiago CB, Afuang WB. 2001. “Preliminary observations on the diel feeding patterns of Nile tilapia *Oreochromis niloticus* (L.) in Laguna de Bay, Philippines, with the aid of the computer model MAXIMS.” [SEAFDEC repository abstract](https://repository.seafdec.org.ph/handle/10862/842).
- World Register of Marine Species: [*Channa striata*, AphiaID 280130](https://www.marinespecies.org/aphia.php?p=taxdetails&id=280130); [*Oreochromis niloticus*, AphiaID 293639](https://www.marinespecies.org/aphia.php?p=taxdetails&id=293639).
- Department of Agriculture–National Fisheries Research and Development Institute, [mudfish locally known as dalag](https://nfrdi.da.gov.ph/2026/03/02/feature-unlocking-aquaculture-potential-of-mudfish-in-muslim-region/).

Food categories are kept as coarse search facets, not claims that a food is always preferred. The published diet summaries are paraphrases. No photos are copied. No effective bait, lure, gear, legal season, protected status, local presence or catch recommendation is supplied. Diet is not treated as bait evidence. Contextual catching advice remains unavailable until independent method evidence and applicable current rules are reviewed.
